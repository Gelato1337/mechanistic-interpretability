You are labelling sparse-autoencoder features of a language model. Each card describes ONE feature: a FACTS header of
computed numbers, the texts of a worked example with the tokens the feature fires on marked as [[token|activation]],
one more passage where it fires, and random fires from other prompts (language prompts and code prompts separately,
each with a few tokens of context). Newlines in tokens are shown as \n. Decide WHAT DETERMINES WHERE IT FIRES.

Classes (pick exactly one):
  position  fires because of WHERE the token sits in the text, not what it is: first token of a passage, first token of a
            sentence or line, right after a newline or punctuation, every token inside a bracket pair, etc. Test: the
            marked tokens have different meanings but the same structural slot. A punctuation mark itself (".", "-",
            closing quote) is a token, not a position; the token AFTER it is a position. Being the 2nd+ piece of a word
            split into several tokens is NOT a position: such a feature is about the words whose pieces it fires on --
            classify it word, concept (e.g. pieces of personal names) or context by what those words have in common, or
            other if they have nothing readable in common.
  token     fires on one exact token string or a small closed set of strings (" the", "(", "def", "\n") wherever they
            occur. Test: the "most frequent tokens" list is dominated by 1-3 strings and the fires are those strings.
  word      fires on one word or a small family of related words in all their surface forms: inflections, capitalisation,
            translations into other languages, split-into-pieces spellings. Test: different strings, same word/lemma.
  concept   fires on many different words that share a MEANING or a grammatical role (numbers, place names, function
            calls, negation, verbs of motion, loop keywords across languages). The token itself carries the property.
            Test: you could name the category and predict new members.
  context   fires on tokens of any kind, whatever they are, whenever the surrounding text is of a certain kind: a topic,
            a language, a programming language, inside a code block, inside a quoted string, inside a comment, formal
            register, a passage about music... The token itself does NOT determine it; the passage does. Test: the marked
            tokens are a mixed bag (nouns, verbs, punctuation, subwords) but all the passages share something.
  other     no readable pattern from this evidence.

Notes. A feature marked <<ALWAYS-ON>> fires in most prompts of every kind; still classify it by what determines its fires
inside a text (it may be context at the coarsest grain, or position, or a token). Coverage numbers help: a feature on
2-5% of tokens is usually token/word/position; one on 30-70% of tokens of a passage is usually context. Do not classify by
what the tokens mean if the placement alone explains the fires. Use the random fires to check that the pattern from the
example holds elsewhere. Check context labels against the corpus rate: a language or programming-language label needs the
feature to fire in most prompts of that language's member domains; a feature firing in under 2% of prompts is topic-
specific, so read the topics of the random fires and name the topic. When the evidence does not settle it, answer other
with low confidence rather than guess.

Answer for every card as one JSON object per line:
{"id": "<card id>", "class": "<position|token|word|concept|context|other>", "what": "<= 12 words: what it responds to",
 "confidence": "high|medium|low", "grain": "<for context/concept only: the kind of passage or category, else null>"}
Output nothing except these JSON lines.
